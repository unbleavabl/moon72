The wiring · both halves

Main plates and thumb plates from below, to scale

Lay each half out like this while you wire: the main plate upside down with the nice!nano glued under its top strip, the thumb plate beside it where it hangs on the hinge, both back edges away from you. Row buses run across the gap above each switch row and step with the column stagger; column buses run down each column on the switch pin nearer the outer edge. Every key's other pin takes a diode to its row bus. Thin numbered wires go from the buses to the nice!nano; the thumb module's five wires run inside the inner thumb hinge, along its axis, to the connector in the tray. Tape each wire's number on at both ends.

Both halves use the same wire numbers to the same pins: each pin follows its finger, so column 0 is the outer pinky column on both. Two things don't mirror, because they are the same parts on both sides: the switch pins (on the right half the column pin is the one on the left, seen from below) and the nice!nano, whose D1–D9 header faces the keys on the left half and the back wall on the right.

How each part connects

Buses, keys, thumb link, power

Switches

Clip every switch in the same way round: pins toward the back edge, LED window toward you (on the thumb plate, "back" is toward the hinge). Then the pin nearer the outer edge of the half takes the column bus and the other one the diode. On the thumb module that rule follows each key's own turn.

Buses

Bare solid copper, soldered at every joint. Columns: straight down each column over the column pins. Rows: across the gap above each switch row, stepping up or down at each column edge with the stagger. Rows 3 and 4 are shorter (6 and 5 keys). Slide sleeve or heat-shrink over the column bus wherever a row crosses it: bare copper on bare copper shorts.

Keys

The diode's plain end on the switch's free pin, its black band end on the row bus. Clip the legs short once both ends are soldered.

Thumb link

Row 5 is the thumb module's own bus. Its five wires (18–22) are the leads of the module-side half of a JST XH pair. They run inside the inner thumb hinge: out of the thumb tray through a slot in its knuckle, along the hollow tube on the hinge axis, and out through a slot in the tray's knuckle into the other half, which lies flat in a pocket in the tray floor. On the axis they only twist when the module tilts: no loop, nothing shows. The tray-side half's leads join row 5 to D9 (wire 6) and the four key columns to the bottom ends of column buses 3–6 (14–17). Wires 18–22 are the connector's own 26 AWG leads: five of them pass the 4.4 mm tube (bundled 3 + 2 through the slots).

Power

Battery red, via its JST-PH pigtail, to the switch's middle pin; the switch's outer pin to B+. Battery black to GND. Off is the switch's other position: the battery only charges over USB with the switch on. Reset: two diagonal legs of the 6×6 button to RST and GND.

nice!nano

Glued under the plate's top strip between its two blocks, component side away from the plate (toward you while wiring), USB-C into the inner wall's notch. Solder the wires from the component side; the 3 mm under the board is for the pin stubs. D10 and D16 are NFC pins: leave them.

Pin reference

nice!nano pins, each half

The boards as they lie while you wire, turned so USB-C is up. They are the same board, so the right half's is not a mirror image: its D1–D9 header ends up on the other side.

Assembly

Before the plates go on

Test with a meter, battery unplugged

  1. Beep setting, probes on B+ and GND: it must not beep. Same for 3V3 and GND.
  2. Diode setting, red probe on the column pin, black on the row pin: for the Q key (row 1, column 1) that is D20 and D5. Press: about 0.5–0.7 V. Release: nothing. Repeat for all 36 keys of the half, thumbs through the mated connector.
  3. A reading with the key released means that row and column touch somewhere. Nothing when pressed usually means a diode is in backwards, or a sleeve is missing nowhere near where you're looking.
  4. Rows against columns, beep setting, nothing pressed: no pair may beep.
  5. Switch: with the battery plugged in, measure B+ to GND: about 3.7–4.2 V in the on position, 0 V in the off position.
  6. Then flash and type every key (a key tester page or wev).
